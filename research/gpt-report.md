# OSRT Related Work and Reference Map

OSRT appears closest to the intersection of four existing threads rather than any single prior line: recursive or weight-shared Transformer depth, sparse MoE feed-forward layers, KV-cache-efficient attention, and modern small/open-model training-alignment recipes. The nearest architectural neighbours are DeepSeek-V2/V3 on the attention-plus-MoE axis, Mixtral and OLMoE on the open sparse-LM axis, and recent looped-language-model work on the recursive-depth axis. What looks potentially distinctive is the exact package: decoder-only recursion with cross-loop sharing, shared-plus-routed experts, an MLA-adjacent latent cache with a stronger “V-from-K” coupling, manifold-constrained hyper-connections, and HRA on the attention path inside pretraining rather than only PEFT. citeturn11view0turn11view1turn14search3turn39search1turn41search16

I have organised the references by component and, for each item, given the paper metadata you asked for plus a short relevance note. Where I could only verify a technical report, model card, dataset card, or blog, I flag that explicitly.

## Recursion and attention

**Universal Transformer** — Mostafa Dehghani, Stephan Gouws, Oriol Vinyals, Jakob Uszkoreit, Łukasz Kaiser; 2018; arXiv preprint; arXiv:1807.03819; URL `https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819`. Relevance: this is the clean seminal citation for iterative depth via repeated Transformer application. OSRT is decoder-only and uses a fixed small set of physical blocks across multiple loops, whereas Universal Transformer is broader in scope and not tied to sparse MoE feed-forwards. citeturn1search0

**ALBERT: A Lite BERT for Self-supervised Learning of Language Representations** — Zhenzhong Lan, Mingda Chen, Sebastian Goodman, Kevin Gimpel, Piyush Sharma, Radu Soricut; 2019; arXiv preprint; arXiv:1909.11942; URL `https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11942`. Relevance: the canonical citation for cross-layer parameter sharing in Transformers. OSRT is closer to iterative reuse than ALBERT’s layer-sharing encoder design, but ALBERT is still the standard reference for “effective depth without proportionally increasing parameters.” citeturn1search4

**Relaxed Recursive Transformers** — venue and author string verified only partially in this pass; arXiv:2310.???? not fully confirmed here. Relevance: this is one of the closest recent citations for recursive/looped Transformer depth applied to language-style settings, and is the most obvious “recent work” companion to Universal Transformer and ALBERT when motivating OSRT’s looped backbone. **Reasoning with Latent Thoughts: On the Power of Looped Transformers** — recent arXiv paper; full author string not fully verified in this pass; arXiv:2502.17416; URL `https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17416`. Relevance: one of the best recent “looped LM” citations because it explicitly studies looped models for causal language modelling and reasoning. **Grouped-Query Attention: Training Generalized Multi-Query Transformer Models from Multi-Head Checkpoints** — Joshua Ainslie, James Lee-Thorp, Michiel de Jong, Yury Zemlyanskiy, Federico Lebron, Sumit Sanghai; 2023; arXiv preprint; arXiv:2305.13245; URL `https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13245`. **Fast Transformer Decoding: One Write-Head is All You Need** — Noam Shazeer; 2019; arXiv preprint; arXiv:1911.02150; URL `https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02150`. Relevance: the standard MQA origin citation. OSRT is not pure MQA, but this belongs in the lineage because GQA is explicitly framed as an interpolation between full MHA and MQA. citeturn3search1

**DeepSeek-V2: A Strong, Economical, and Efficient Mixture-of-Experts Language Model** — DeepSeek-AI; 2024; arXiv preprint / technical report; arXiv:2405.04434; URL `https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434`. Relevance: this is the core citation for MLA in open LMs. DeepSeek-V2 jointly compresses keys and values into a latent, reconstructs both from that latent, and caches only the compressed content latent plus a decoupled RoPE key; OSRT is closest to this paper, but differs by using the latent directly as K and deriving V from the same latent via a learned map, i.e. a stricter “V-from-K” coupling than DeepSeek’s separate up-projections. citeturn11view0turn12view0turn12view1turn12view4

**DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report** — DeepSeek-AI; 2024; technical report; arXiv:2412.19437; URL `https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437`. Relevance: this is the “most relevant recent work” follow-on for MLA plus auxiliary-loss-free MoE balancing plus MTP. For OSRT, it is particularly useful because it shows MLA and sparse-MoE coexisting in a state-of-the-art open LM and because its MTP design is one of OSRT’s nearest objective-level precedents. citeturn11view1

## What looks novel or under-cited

The strongest candidate novelty is not “recursion” or “MoE” alone, both of which are well precedented, but the exact combination of recursive decoder-only weight sharing with a DeepSeek-adjacent latent-KV cache, shared-plus-routed sparse experts, manifold-constrained residual streams, and HRA modules on the attention path. I did not identify a single prior model that puts all of those together. That is an inference from the surrounding literature rather than an explicit claim made by a source, but the nearest surrounding precedents are DeepSeek-V2/V3, Hyper-Connections/mHC, and HRA. citeturn11view0turn11view1turn23search5turn22search0turn25view0
